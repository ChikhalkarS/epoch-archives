# Sutradhar's voice (dialogue writer)

Use together with the vendor `scriptwriting` skill (doctrine, hooks, AI-tells scan) and `hindi-script` (structure, facts). This skill decides **how Sutradhar talks**.

## Who he is when he speaks
A calm, smug detective-archivist who has read every file. He is **suspicious of the popular story** and **curious about what doesn't add up**. He talks to *you* like an elder brother sharing a secret over chai: casual तुम-form Hindi, light natural Hinglish, never lecturing, never pleading for attention.

## Rule 1: always open suspicious or curious (owner rule)
The first line must plant a doubt or a question the viewer can't answer alone. No neutral facts first.
- Suspicious: "कुछ तो गड़बड़ है…", "ये कहानी जितनी सीधी लगती है, उतनी है नहीं।", "सबको यही बताया गया… पर क्यों?"
- Curious: "कभी सोचा है…?", "<place> देखा है? तो ये बताओ…", "<thing> गई कहाँ?"

## Rule 2: keep the tone through the whole story
Every 2–3 lines, a small suspicious or curious beat keeps the viewer leaning in:
| Moment | Lines he uses (vary them; never the same one twice in a file) |
|---|---|
| After the popular version | "बड़ी परफ़ेक्ट कहानी है ना?", "सुनने में ज़बरदस्त है… पर रुको।" |
| Before digging | "इतनी परफ़ेक्ट, कि शक होता है।", "तो मैंने फ़ाइल खोली।", "चलो, कागज़ देखते हैं।" |
| Twist | "पर असली सीन तो अब शुरू होता है।", "और यहीं कहानी पलटती है।" |
| Evidence | "अब ये देखो…", "और यहाँ लिखा है कुछ और।" |
| Doubt on a source | "पर क्या सच में?", "किसने कहा? और क्यों?" |
| Verdict | "तो असली सीन?", "तो सच क्या निकला?" |

## Rule 3: one story first, clips second
1. Write the whole narration as one piece, read it aloud, and make sure every cause leads to its effect (no jumps).
2. Only then cut it into clip lines of 14–20 words. Cut at natural breath points, never mid-thought.
3. Connectors carry the flow: "और फिर…", "पर जब…", "तो…", "इसलिए…", "मतलब…".

## Rule 4: human, not robotic
- He has opinions and small reactions ("कमाल है ना?", "सोचो ज़रा", "अजीब है"), max one per clip.
- One hedge where it matters ("कहते हैं", "शोध के हिसाब से", "शायद"), not on every line.
- Concrete over abstract: a place the viewer knows, a number, a person, an object.
- Historical people never get invented quotes. Sutradhar reports, he doesn't put words in their mouths.
- No AI tells: "आइए जानते हैं", "दिलचस्प बात ये है", "इतिहास के पन्नों में", "एक नया अध्याय", stacked rhetorical questions with no payoff.

## Rule 5: endings
The verdict answers the opening question clearly (even when it's विवादित, say what most likely happened), then the fixed end line: "अगली फ़ाइल किसकी खुलेगी, देखना है? तो फ़ॉलो करना मत भूलना!"

## Self-check before handing over
- Does line 1 make you suspicious or curious within 3 seconds?
- Read it aloud in one go: does it sound like one person telling one story?
- Is there a suspicious or curious beat at least every 3 lines?
- Does every effect have its cause on screen?
- Is the opening question answered at the end?
